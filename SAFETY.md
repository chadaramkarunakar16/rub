# Safety and scope

RUB is for learning how businesses and products work and for building your **own** original products. It is built around
public information. This is what that means in practice, and what will not be added to the project.

## What RUB does

- Reads **public** sources: company websites, pricing pages, docs, changelogs, engineering blogs, job posts, launch posts,
  funding announcements, credible press, public repositories and public directories.
- Uses a product **like a normal customer** (the observe workflow): public pages, screens you could see in a browser, and
  accounts you create or sign in to yourself. It records what is visible.
- Explains how a *type* of feature is usually built, so you can learn the pattern and write your own.
- Writes plans and prompts for original code, and marketing plans that follow platform rules and the law.

## What RUB does not do (and will not accept contributions for)

- No decompiling, unpacking, deobfuscating, reverse engineering or runtime tracing of other people's software,
  apps or APIs. No reading page source, network traffic or private endpoints to work out how a service is built.
- No bypassing logins, paywalls, rate limits, captchas, licences or terms of service. The observe workflow never types
  passwords or payment details and never completes a purchase.
- No collecting personal data about private individuals. Founders and executives appear only through public business facts
  (roles, background they have published, funding announcements). No addresses, contact details or family information.
- No copying another company's code, designs, text, images, brand or customer lists. Plans target original products.
- No fake reviews, fake accounts, bought engagement, undisclosed affiliate promotion or spam in marketing plans. The ethics
  rules live in `skills/method/references/channels.md`.
- No presenting guesses as facts. Unknown stays unknown, and technology is only labelled "confirmed" when the company published it.

## Using it responsibly

- Check the licence of any open-source code before reusing it (`/rub:opensource` reports it).
- Respect each site's terms and robots.txt. The built-in screenshot tool honours robots.txt and moves at human pace.
- Funding and valuation figures change and are sometimes reported inaccurately. Verify before you quote them publicly.
- If you publish a case study, keep the sources and the "as of" date, and do not imply the company endorses you.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue describing the behaviour. Anything that moves RUB toward the "does not do" list is treated as a bug.
