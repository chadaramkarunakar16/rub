# Marketing channels and the ethics rules

Use this as the checklist of channels to research. Always confirm what is working **now** with dated,
recent sources; channels change fast and this list is a starting point, not evidence.

| Channel | Works best for | Early-stage version | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short-form video (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | Visual, demo-able products; consumer and prosumer | One honest build or demo video a day; show the result in the first 2 seconds | Needs volume and consistency; vanity views without signups |
| Communities (Reddit, Discord, Slack groups, forums) | Niche audiences with a clear pain | Answer questions helpfully; share the product only where relevant and allowed | Each community's self-promotion rules; disclose you're the maker |
| Founder-led onboarding and sales | Anything early; B2B especially | Personally onboard the first 20-50 users; learn their words | Doesn't scale; turn insights into onboarding and FAQs |
| Creator and affiliate partnerships | Products creators already use or teach | Recurring commission, no upfront fee, free account for creators | Disclosure rules; quality of referred users |
| AI assistants and answer engines | Products people ask about ("best X for Y") | Clear comparison pages, docs, FAQs and genuine third-party mentions that assistants can cite | No guaranteed placement; measure with referral data and branded search |
| SEO and programmatic pages | Many long-tail use cases (templates, "X for Y") | 20-50 genuinely useful pages for real use cases | Thin or auto-generated pages can be penalised |
| Launch platforms and directories | Developer, AI and productivity tools | Product Hunt, Hacker News (Show HN), AI directories, YC Launch if applicable | One-day spikes; prepare onboarding first |
| LinkedIn / X founder posting | B2B, AI, founder audiences | Build in public: numbers, lessons, demos | Slow compounding; consistency matters |
| Email and newsletters | Retention and activation | Onboarding sequence; sponsor small niche newsletters | Consent and unsubscribe required |
| Student and education plans | Tools students use daily | Free or discounted plan verified by school email | Abuse controls; conversion after graduation |
| Referral loops | Products with sharing built in | Give-get credits; share links on outputs | Fraud controls |
| India-specific | Indian users and SMBs | ₹ pricing with UPI, WhatsApp communities and broadcast, regional-language content, college ambassador programmes | GST invoices for B2B; consent for WhatsApp messages |

## Ethics rules every plan must follow

1. **Be honest about who you are.** Disclose that you're the maker when posting in communities. No fake
   accounts, sock puppets or "I just found this tool" posts.
2. **No fake proof.** No fake reviews, testimonials, user counts, screenshots or "as seen in" logos. Ask real
   users for reviews instead.
3. **No bought engagement.** No buying upvotes, followers, comments or review swaps.
4. **Follow each platform's rules.** Read community and subreddit rules before posting; respect self-promotion limits.
5. **DMs only where welcome.** Message people who publicly asked for help with the problem, a few a day,
   personally written, and stop if they don't reply. No mass automated DMs or scraped lists.
6. **Email and messaging law.** Only email or WhatsApp people who opted in; always include unsubscribe.
   Follow anti-spam and data-protection law where your users are (for India, the DPDP Act; for the US, CAN-SPAM;
   for the EU/UK, GDPR).
7. **Disclose paid and affiliate relationships.** Creators must label sponsored or affiliate content as
   required (ASCI guidelines in India, FTC in the US).
8. **Copy tactics, not assets.** Learn from a competitor's strategy; never copy their brand, copy text,
   designs, or customer lists, and never imply you're them.
